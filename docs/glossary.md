# Throughline — Glossary

Domain terms used across the Throughline build spec. Definitions draw on the product/technical context in `docs/PRODUCT_SPEC.md` (Part II). Sorted alphabetically.

- **AICP** — The Association of Independent Commercial Producers. Its standardized commercial bid form (production cost sections A–W) is the de-facto structure for agency/commercial budgets; Throughline authors, exports, and round-trips it as a first-class format.
- **AICP bid (sections A–W)** — The lettered section structure of an AICP commercial bid (e.g. pre-production, shooting crew, locations, equipment, post). Budgets must author and export in this section form and round-trip losslessly on core fields.
- **ATL / BTL** — Above-the-Line vs Below-the-Line. ATL covers creative principals (writer, director, producers, principal cast); BTL covers the rest of production labor and expenses. The budget top sheet splits ATL / BTL-production / Post / Other, and tax-incentive eligibility often treats them differently (ATL is frequently capped).
- **Breakdown** — The scene-by-scene decomposition of a script into tagged production elements (cast, props, wardrobe, locations, vehicles, SFX/VFX, stunts, animals, sound, set dressing). Throughline drafts it with AI (confidence + provenance) and lets a human confirm each tag.
- **Call sheet** — The daily document sent to cast and crew listing who is called, when and where, which scenes shoot, plus weather, locations, and logistics. Throughline auto-populates it from the graph and tracks per-recipient delivery and acknowledgment.
- **COI** — Certificate of Insurance. Proof of coverage (typically an additional-insured endorsement with a liability limit and issue/expiry dates) required per location; Throughline stores it in the location document vault and alerts on expiry.
- **Committed cost** — Money contractually obligated but not yet paid — chiefly approved, open purchase orders (POs). One of the inputs to EFC; becomes an actual once the invoice is matched (3-way match).
- **Company move** — Relocating the production unit from one shooting location to another (with move time and travel time). It consumes shooting time, bends labor rules, and is reflected on both the schedule and the call sheet.
- **Coogan account** — A blocked trust account into which a legally mandated percentage of a minor performer's earnings is deposited (required in CA, NY, IL, LA, NM, with differing percentages and custodian thresholds). Part of the per-jurisdiction child-labor compliance tables.
- **CRDT** — Conflict-free Replicated Data Type. The data structure (via Yjs) behind multiplayer editing and offline-first sync: concurrent edits to the stripboard/budget merge deterministically without a central lock. The CRDT is the editing layer; the event log is the truth layer.
- **CQRS** — Command Query Responsibility Segregation. Writes append events; separate async projectors materialize read views. Paired with event sourcing, it powers reactive propagation, audit, and Time Machine.
- **Dailies** — The raw footage (and, in Throughline's Phase 3 sense, its derived intelligence: transcription, diarization, alignment, shot detection, VLM tags) captured on a shoot day and reviewed/indexed. Feeds searchable post handoff.
- **DOOD (Day-Out-of-Days)** — The grid tracking, per cast member, which shooting days they work, hold, travel, start, or finish. Derived from the schedule; correctly distinguishes work / hold / travel and drives cast cost.
- **DPR (Daily Production Report)** — The end-of-day report summarizing what was actually shot vs planned (scenes, pages, hours, delays). Auto-drafted from on-set taps and actuals; filing it through the product is part of the North Star metric.
- **EFC** — Estimate at Completion (also "estimated final cost"). The projected total cost of an account. Fixed formula: **EFC = Actuals + Committed + ETC**; and **Variance = Budget − EFC**. (Appendix B labels the abbreviation "estimate-to-complete"; ETC is the estimate-to-complete component within it.)
- **ETC** — Estimate To Complete. The forecast of remaining cost still to be incurred on an account. The forward-looking input to EFC (EFC = Actuals + Committed + ETC).
- **Event sourcing** — Persisting state as an append-only log of events rather than mutable rows. The events table is the durable source of truth; projections are rebuildable, versioning is free, and any past state is reconstructable (Time Machine, blame, branches).
- **Exhibit G** — The SAG-AFTRA daily time report: per-performer report/dismiss times, meals, travel, work-status codes, minor flag, and penalty fields, signed by the performer. It is the feed for Daily Hot Costs.
- **Faithfulness** — A RAG/grounded-Q&A metric measuring whether an answer is fully supported by the retrieved context (no hallucinated claims). A hard eval gate: faithfulness ≥ 0.90 or the system abstains / routes to a human.
- **FDX** — Final Draft's XML screenplay file format (`.fdx`). A primary script import/export format; round-trips without core-field loss via deterministic parsers, never AI.
- **Forced call** — Calling a crew or cast member back to work before their required rest period (turnaround) has elapsed. It triggers penalty pay; Throughline flags it predictively at schedule time and computes it at timecard time.
- **Fringe** — Payroll add-ons applied to wages (union pension & health, taxes, workers' comp, etc.), defined by name/rate/unit/cap and applied to fringe ranges. Fringes flow proportionally when the underlying wages change.
- **Hold day** — A day on which a booked cast member does not work but must still be paid to keep them available. The cast-spread optimizer minimizes hold days to cut cost.
- **Meal penalty** — Escalating penalty pay owed when a crew/cast member is not given a required meal break within the allowed interval. Counted per person per week; predictive at schedule time, actual at timecard time.
- **Movie Magic (.mmb / .mmsp)** — Entertainment Partners' industry-standard scheduling (`.mmsp`) and budgeting (`.mmb`) file formats. Throughline interoperates by round-tripping these files losslessly on core fields (account codes, fringes, globals) rather than fighting the format.
- **Optimality gap** — In the scheduling optimizer eval, the percentage distance between the solver's solution and the best-known/optimal baseline for an instance. Target mean ≤ 10%; tracked alongside the fraction solved to proven optimality.
- **pass@k** — An eval consistency metric: the fraction of tasks solved in at least one of k trials (one success suffices). Used for capability evals.
- **pass^k** — An eval consistency metric: the fraction of tasks solved in all k trials — a stricter, customer-facing measure of reliable consistency.
- **Sides** — The condensed pages of just the scenes being shot on a given day (half-letter format), optionally character-filtered. Auto-generated from the call sheet's scenes in shooting order and distributed with per-recipient watermarking.
- **Spread cost** — A cost spread/allocated across multiple shooting days or accounts (rather than booked to a single day). Part of the private cost intelligence the event log accumulates over productions.
- **Stripboard** — The visual shooting schedule: one strip per scene, orderable and groupable by day, from which the schedule and DOOD derive. Generated from confirmed breakdown elements under constraints and manually re-orderable.
- **Time Machine** — The event-log scrubber feature: a timeline slider that reconstructs the schedule/budget at any past event, shows who changed each figure and why (blame), and restores any prior state as a branch. Exposed on the API via `GET /v1/projects/{id}/graph?at={eventId}`.
- **Top sheet** — The one-page budget summary rolling up all accounts (ATL → BTL → Post → Other → fringes → contingency). The headline number line producers watch; recomputes live as the graph changes.
- **Turnaround** — The mandated rest period between a crew/cast member's wrap and their next call. Thresholds vary by union and location context (e.g. studio 12h vs location 10/11h); invading it triggers forced-call penalties. Tracked as a per-person running counter.
- **Worked hours vs elapsed hours** — Two distinct quantities tracked per person per day. Worked hours are compensable time on the clock; elapsed hours are wall-clock span (including unpaid gaps). Modeling them separately reconciles almost every cross-union overtime, meal, and turnaround rule.
